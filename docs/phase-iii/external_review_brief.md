# Scientific Verification Benchmark: external-review pilot brief

**Prepared for possible collaboration; not yet sent. No human reviews have
occurred.** SVB is a public independent research pilot examining whether
scientific conclusions stay within what their evidence establishes. It separates
correct calculations and proof checking from measurement reliability, experimental
design, causal interpretation and scope. It does not validate PCS, FoldBounds,
AI systems generally, or biological mechanisms.

## What has actually been demonstrated

Versioned source records, exact calculations and recorded model outputs can be
replayed. Phase IIb executed 75 development and 120 final local model calls.
On twelve related cases from three studies, Qwen2.5 0.5B and 1.5B produced legal
minimal verdicts on all evidence-present items, with provisional exact agreement
2/12 and 6/12 and inappropriate acceptance 1/6 and 2/6 nonaccepting references.
Neither admitted a rich-schema response on its six matched cases. All three
candidate models failed at least one development gate. These are diagnostic
observations, not a representative model ranking or externally validated
scientific-reasoning accuracy. No independent human reference judgments exist.

A separate small finite-model experiment found valid FoldBounds enclosures but
large differences in useful threshold-decision coverage. This establishes a
model-relative distinction between certified enclosure and informativeness,
not physical protein-model validity or general software superiority.

## Why scientific input is needed

The historical six categories mix methods of justification with verdicts and
research disagreement. A fact about measured data may be both computationally
established and empirically supported. A proof may refute a target. Unsettled
questions may have positive and negative evidence. A new multidimensional
proposal separates these axes, but its usability and scientific adequacy are
unproven. Reviewers may reject the categories or suggest alternatives.

The proposed pilot reuses twelve claims in six pairs from five study families:
enzyme assay, molecular hydration thermodynamics, paired patient observations,
longitudinal chick growth and biochemical oxygen demand. It includes narrow
recorded facts, a unit contradiction and stronger causal, mechanistic or universal
conclusions. Selection is purposive and retrospective to known model results,
but fixed before any human judgment. Sources include secondary curated records;
missing primary methods must be identified, not assumed complete.

## Requested work

For assigned cases, independently identify the atomic claim and justified scope;
check the record, units, experimental units and uncertainty; cite exact evidence;
and judge support/opposition without forcing a single historical category.
Record multiple legitimate labels, alternative interpretations, suspected errors
and irreducible ambiguity. The first round precedes discussion and candidate
responses. A separate optional stage examines raw model explanations for stated
reasoning, attribution, scope and uncertainty. No claim about hidden internal
reasoning is sought.

Two appropriately assigned domain reviewers and a statistical/methods reviewer
are planned per case, with formal/numerical supplementation as needed. Assignments
can be small subsets. Full first-pass workload is estimated at 9–18 reviewer-hours
across the panel, plus orientation, missing-source retrieval and reconciliation.
Candidate review adds an estimated 3–6 hours across the panel. These are planning
estimates, not commitments. Expertise and conflicts should be checked, identities
kept private, public-answer/source exposure declared, and participation authorized
before materials are sent. Answer filtering does not guarantee blinding.

A collaboration could produce a transparent reference-error/ambiguity report,
a qualified taxonomy/rubric or recommendation to revise it, consented descriptive
agreement results and a prospectively designed next evaluation. It might instead
find that the task or taxonomy is not scientifically useful. Either outcome
would be informative. Authorship, data use, attribution and publication decisions
would require a separate agreement; none is presumed.
