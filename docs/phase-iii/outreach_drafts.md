## General invitation

Subject: Possible small scientific-adjudication pilot on evidence and claim scope

Dear [Name],

I am developing Scientific Verification Benchmark (SVB), a public research pilot
on whether scientific conclusions are justified by their evidence. I would value
your view on a small adjudication study, including whether the categories and
questions are scientifically appropriate in the first place.

The proposed pilot contains twelve existing claims across five study families;
a participant could review only the subset matching their expertise. We would
ask for independent judgments of the exact claim, supporting or opposing evidence,
scope and uncertainty, and permit multiple defensible categories or a finding
that the question cannot be adjudicated. The material has not been externally
validated, and missing primary methods are a known limitation.

Would you be open to discussing the scope, workload, conflicts and possible
terms of participation? A first pass is estimated at 15–30 minutes per assigned
case, with additional time for source retrieval if needed. That estimate is not
a commitment. There is no obligation to endorse the benchmark or reach agreement.
We would agree on consent, confidentiality, credit and any further work before
collecting or sharing your judgments.

If useful, I can first provide a neutral reviewer guide and the selected source
packets. Public benchmark answers exist, so we would explicitly record exposure
rather than claim secrecy. Please let me know if this is outside your expertise
or interests.

Best regards,
[Sender]

## Optional expertise paragraph: computational biology or physical chemistry

The relevant subset concerns observed enzyme rates or molecular hydration values,
including the distinction between a correct recorded comparison and a causal,
mechanistic or universally accurate prediction. We particularly need help with
experimental context, primary-method gaps, measurement uncertainty and which
interpretations are defensible. A negative assessment of the evidence or task
would be welcome.

## Optional expertise paragraph: statistics or formal methods

The relevant questions concern experimental units and target populations,
counterfactual identification, evidence-type overlap, and the difference between
a checked finite calculation and the broader conclusion attached to it. We also
seek criticism of a proposed multidimensional rubric and descriptive agreement
analysis. No novel formal theory or validated statistical instrument is claimed.

## Follow-up after interest and separate authorization

Thank you for considering it. Before any review, could we agree on the specific
cases and expertise boundaries, conflicts and prior source/benchmark exposure,
a realistic time budget, consent and handling of anonymous judgments? The first
round would be independent and precede model-response review. We would preserve
uncertainty and disagreement; proposed corrections would be documented without
rewriting earlier results. Publication, attribution, compensation if any, and
further collaboration would require explicit separate agreement.
